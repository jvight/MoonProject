## 1. The first hour as a player feels it

### 0.4.1 today
| Time (human) | What happens | How it feels |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Wake (`playthrough-01-awake`) | **The best frame in the game.** Earth, the Peak's red blink and a long shadow. But home is already fully lit: windows, porch lamps, a neon ring by the tower. Nothing at home is left to discover. We also see 07 from **behind**: two amber tail lamps and a slot read as a two-eyed face. The real one-lens eye, the "aww" of ruling 8, is never seen opening. |
| 0:30 | Drive 55 m to the depot (`01b-the-depot`) | Calm, but the ground is one flat pale plate. The depot is bright white and showroom clean, with nothing to read at 30 m. |

| 5–8 | Dig the cassette player, tow it home (`04`, `05-at-the-shelf`) | Towing is the best "every touch sings" moment so far. | 8–10 | Tower L1 (`07-tower-awake`) | **No tension.** L1 costs 0 metal, 1 wiring and 1 optics, while the player holds 12/5/2 after one site. The "awake" tower is a lamp post shorter than the lander, so the heart of the base doesn't read as one. |
| 10–20 | Tilly: find her, gather parts, stitch, bring her home (`10`, `11-tilly-greets`) | **The emotional high point** of the hour: a friend wakes. At home, though, she hovers on 07's back and merges into its silhouette (`11`), so you can't see who came home. |

**Verdict.** The moments (wake frame, tow, Tilly, the leap) are good. The hour around them has no order, no
tension and no "why". Home is a lit prop yard from minute one. Materials stop mattering after site one: the whole
tower costs 0 metal, and metal costs for every kit total 10 while the basin holds 98 units of salvage, about 3.5× the
needs against ruling 5's 2×. New systems arrive in a burst at the end instead of one per trip.

### The M3-16 plan
It fixes the owner's three complaints at the root: a dark base, one job per place, and a power spine. Walked as a
player, the plan still has five problems:
- **The order will collapse.** The steps are gated only by tower levels, and the tower is gated only by materials.
  With today's costs (whole tower 0/4/5) and the depot's 12/5/2, a player can buy L1, then fix Tilly and reach L2
  within one trip of optics, or skip Tilly entirely. The "what woke and why" chain then turns into "I paid".
- **The first ten minutes risk being silent or wordy, nothing in between.** If every dormant thing answers the sonar
  with "not yet" plus a line, and every first approach shows a name and a job, a player who circles home in minute two
  reads about 14 lines: a wall of UI. If nothing answers, the dark base is a mystery with no handle.
- **The 90 min estimate is padding-shaped.** Steps 0–8 hold about 15 min of bot content plus two more sites (the
  Kestrel and the drill), which is about 50–60 human minutes. That's fine. Don't stretch it to hit 90.
- **The bench's new job breaks ruling 14 as written:** "07 scans blueprints and they appear here". Who pins the sheet?
- **The lift wakes last (~90 min)** although it sits in every frame from minute zero. That's acceptable only if the jam
  reads clearly as "broken, later", and today it does not (M3-15 §2).

## 2. Holes in M3-16

**Pacing between rewards.**
- Steps 0–4 pay off every 5–8 min, which is good.
- Steps 5–7 (32–90) contain the longest drives (255 m out, 616 m home) and the fewest home changes: the bay wakes at
  32 and nothing at home changes again until L3.
- Every return trip needs something new at home: a shelf slot lamp per relic, a sheet on the board per blueprint, a
  friend's corner, a lit cable from each relay.
- The spec lists these elsewhere (DESIGN "home shows it too") but doesn't schedule them into the steps. Give each
  step row a "home changes" column.

**What the player does while the base is dark (steps 0–1).**
- The only verbs are drive, look and ping, and the only target is the depot. That's right: short and focused.
- But the spec contradicts itself. "Only the dock and 07's eye are lit", yet the tower is "a dark broken stub with
  a lit port hopper".
- Resolve it on purpose: **three warm points** (the dock, 07's eye, and one slow amber pilot blink on the tower port
  that rhymes with the Peak's red blink). That gives the player one obvious "what's that light?" before any text.

**Motivation without text, step by step.**
- **Step 1 (depot): clear.** The ping answers.
- **Step 2 (feed the tower): weak.** Why would a new player bring metal to a pole? Fix: the dock's own ground cable
  runs to the tower stub and glows dimly. The world shows "the dock is plugged into that".
- **Step 3 (Tilly): clear.** A chirp and a pillar.
- **Step 4 (memory): clear** once the shelf wakes on arrival. But the depot's heart already holds a relic (the cassette
  player is dug in step 1 of today's golden path). That conflicts with step 4 "first memory". Move it (see P1).
- **Step 5 (bay): broken order.** The bay offers Hover-Jump before the player has seen a reason to jump (see P2).
- **Step 7 (relays): unclear.** Nothing at home asks for a relay. The masts need to be visible from the lookout or
  the dock as dark silhouettes first.

**Failure and confusion cases the spec must answer.**
1. *Off-path player.* They skip the depot and drive to the garage. They dig the duck and the Cradle tube before the
   tower is awake. Which steps break?
   - Rule: anything on open ground works in any order, only power gates, and the "next" line adapts to the real state.
   - Add a scrambled-route Playthrough variant.
2. *A blueprint found while the bay is dark.* Where does it live? Answer it visibly: the tube rides on 07's back
   (clipped to the stripe or the Cradle) until it reaches the board. A reason to go home, and ruling 11.
3. *A blueprint for a kit that doesn't exist.* M3-16 puts the Wider Sonar tube at Bell's terminus, but there is no
   Wider Sonar upgrade asset, and Wide Sonar is on the ROADMAP for 0.7. A found sheet you can never build breaks the
   promise. Either ship the kit in 0.5 or put the Boost Coils (or nothing) there.
4. *Trying the chasm early.* Ruling 10 covers the exit. Make the failed attempt a teaching beat: 07 looks across, the
   far light pulses, and that light later becomes the Hover-Jump sheet's illustration.
5. *Resting at the dock with nothing done.* "Resting saves the day" has nothing to show in step 0. Make the dock's
   first rest the wake-up itself.
6. *Too much stillness.* The "next" line shows whenever 07 stops. That collides with pillar 6 ("stillness is a
   reward") and with the M3-15 §5 stargazing beat, which fades the HUD on the same stop (see P8).

**What makes coming home feel good.** Nothing in the spec is a homecoming *ritual*. Today the dock is a parking spot
with a glow. Coming home should have a beat:
- the radio warms as you cross the tower's circle;
- Tilly or Bell come out to meet 07;
- parking on the dock ends the trip with a short wide shot of home's warm points, everything you brought lit;
- the tower "listens" and one new faint signal rises on the horizon (see P6).

That beat is both the coming-home reward and the next trip's hook.

## 3. The core loop's fun

**Is salvage → craft → explore satisfying?**
- **Salvage: pleasant but repetitive.** The melody carries it. But six equal cuts with no surprise is a chore by the
  third site. Each site's last cut should open its heart (the relic or the blueprint glints as the last panel falls).
  Then the cuts feel like digging toward something.
- **Craft: unearned.** Materials never run short, metal is dead after site one, and the bay offered everything at once.
  M3-16's blueprints fix "everything at once". The economy still makes "craft" a confirmation click, not a goal.
- **Explore: the strongest part,** when there is a visible promise (the chasm, Bell's light). It is weakest between
  promises: the long plain in `16-trail-to-the-canyon` and the 616 m drive home.

**The "one more trip" hook.** It should be **the light you can see but haven't reached**. DESIGN's reason #4 says
so, but today there is no moment that shows the player the next light. Make the end of every trip show one: the
dock's listening sweep raises a pillar on the horizon (a site, a friend's chirp or a dark relay) before the player
can decide to quit. The player goes to bed knowing exactly where they'll drive tomorrow.

**What brings a cozy player back tomorrow.**
- In order: a friend waiting (Tilly and Bell need idle lives you can *see* from the dock), the half-lit home ("one
  more window"), and the named next light.
- Moon drift (M3-08) moved to 0.6 and that's right. A **one-line slice** of it costs almost nothing: on loading a save,
  Tilly has found one new glint near home overnight and flies to it once. It keeps the habit without the system.
