# Story v3: the proof, the other station, and the silence (0.5 → 1.0)

> Author: the story & flow session, for the owner. It replaces `story-v2.md` after the owner's review (memory
> `story-logic`):
> 1. the elders' trail alone isn't enough of a pull;
> 2. no sentimental human-to-robot story: people treat robots as tools;
> 3. the elders are all at rest;
> 4. keep the silence thread and resolve it logically.
>
> It is consistent with game design's machine-ecology rules (`0.6-living-moon.md` §1a, design/0.6 be917674): every
> machine has a power source, a goal and a reason. Tone: cozy, melancholic, hopeful. No villains, no horror, no
> cutscene exposition, no voice.

## 1. The arc in one page
**The premise, corrected.**
- 2047: Lumen Station, a four-person radio-relay and research outpost near the Moon's south pole, is evacuated in a
  solar storm.
- On Earth, the program review closes the station. The official verdict: the site is **"not worth returning to"**.
  There was no evidence of usable water, so a permanent base couldn't pay for itself.
- **The verdict was wrong, and the proof is still on the Moon.**

**The central pull: carry the proof home.**
- **What Ines found.** In the station's last weeks, Ines Kalani, geologist and mission lead, found signs of a large
  water-ice reservoir under the region: the base the program wanted.
  - Her drill cores were "anomalous". Her one blue pebble was a hydrated bead from the deep core, and she kept it.
  - **Meridian Station**, the other station beyond the rim, had radar soundings that matched her cores.
- **Why it never reached Earth.** The data was due to go home through Lumen's great dish on the night of the storm.
  The storm broke the dish first. The crew left on the emergency ascent with nothing but themselves (no samples, no
  drives), and the evidence stayed scattered across the instruments, machines and caches of both stations.
- **Why it still counts.** Back on Earth, Ines argued for a return without the data, and lost.
- **What 07 does.** 07 runs the station's operational **Lumen Recovery Protocol**:
  1. restore power;
  2. restore the relay chain;
  3. restore the uplink;
  4. **transmit the station archive.**

  The archive includes Ines's survey. The player gathers its pieces region by region, learning what they prove,
  and sends them home.
- **The pull, in one line:** *the Moon was written off, and you are carrying the reason to come back.* It's
  Subnautica's pull turned hopeful: you're not escaping a planet, you're giving people a reason to return to it.

**The second station: Meridian.**
- An uncrewed prospecting station run from Earth by another agency, the **Tycho Consortium**, high on the Rim
  Terraces (near-eternal sunlight, a clear Earth view).
- **How the two stations cooperated:**
  - Meridian's traffic went home through Lumen's dish and Meridian's own relay satellite, **Kestrel-3**.
  - The two crews' scientists shared data: Ines on the Moon, and Meridian's science lead Dr. Lena Moreau on Earth.
- **What the storm did:** it dropped Kestrel-3 into the basin and broke Lumen's dish. Meridian lost its only link
  to Earth. The Consortium couldn't reach it and wrote it off.
- **What happened next:** Meridian's arrays are high and still sunlit, so **its machines never stopped.** For
  decades they ran their last task queue (prospect, sound, sample, store) with nobody reading the results.
- **The decisive part of the proof** is in Meridian's vault: decades of soundings mapping the whole reservoir.

**The silence.**
- The mare's comms dish archived the crew's group channel for twenty years. After evacuation, the four of them kept
  writing to each other on the station's address: Ines fighting the review, Theo, Kenji, and Ro.
- Then the archive stops in 2067.
- **The logical cause:** the dish's own array dusted over after the sweeper that cleaned it broke. The dish lost
  power and stopped listening. Nobody on Earth stopped.

**The ending.**
1. The great dish on the Peak is restored. 07 runs the protocol's last step and transmits the station archive: the
   crew's logs and **the survey**.
2. **Who hears it first:** Ro. She has broadcast her show "Lumen After Dark" every Sunday for decades, through an
   amateur radio club's dish, at the station's old frequency, out of habit and as a protest against the closure.
   - That broadcast is also **what woke 07** at the start: relay.2 caught it, 06 relayed it, and the protocol
     started.
   - Her club's dish is the one listening when the station answers.
3. **The reply,** human to human, station to Earth:
   - first Ro's line on the channel: "Lumen, this is Amadi. We read you. Ines is on her way to the club.";
   - then, the last line of the game: "Lumen Station, this is Houston. Survey received. A crewed return is being
     planned."
4. **The silence resolves:** the station went deaf, and Earth never stopped talking. The radio now plays Ro's show
   live.
5. **After the ending:** the channel carries Earth's preparations, a letter at a time (the mission's name, the crew
   chosen, Ines on the review board). Free roam continues.

## 3. The two stations' machines
### 3.0 The logic base (carried over from v2 §3.1; `0.6-living-moon.md` §1a)
- **Light:** Lumen sits at a near-polar site.
  - The Sun skims the horizon. A lunar day lasts two Earth weeks in reality, or 2 hours of play if the owner picks the
    moving Sun (L1).
  - High ground (the arrays, the Peak, Meridian's terraces) is almost always sunlit; the Shadowed Crater and the lava
    tube never are.
  - Earthshine lights the world but never charges anything.
- **Dust** settles on any panel nobody cleans, and a dusty array makes less power.
- **Power sources:**
  - own solar skin;
  - an induction pad fed by a clean, sunlit array;
  - the station grid;
  - swappable cells;
  - **sealed isotope cells**, which the crew put only where the Sun can't reach: the storm shelter and the dish
    station's vault.
- **The elders are all at rest.** The fault beacon runs on the dish station's own array and pad. The shelter's lights
  and grow lamp run on its sealed cell, on the automated garden timer. No elder is involved in either.

**Why Meridian's vault still filled for decades, despite safe mode:**
- Safe mode triggers on the loss of a **command link**, and for the station's own machines the command link is
  **Meridian's local scheduler**, not Earth.
- Earth was lost in 2047, but the scheduler kept looping its last survey plan in near-eternal sunlight. So **machines
  in range of Meridian's comms node** (the sorters, the wipers, the cable climbers) kept working, and the soundings
  kept piling into the vault.
- **Field machines out of that range went to safe mode and parked:**
  - the flock's prospector on the mare;
  - the ice tappers, once the storm cut their crater cable and node link.
- **Waking them takes contact, not power:** 07 repairs their comms node, and machines in its range resume their last
  queue (§1a.6, rule 4).

## 4. How the story is told (no sentimental human-to-robot lines)
**The owner's rule:**
- People talk to people.
- Machines get operational text: protocols, task queues, manifests and maintenance notes.
- 07 has character only through its behaviour (head turns, chirps, how it pauses), never through anyone's words to it.

| Channel | What it carries | Who to whom |
|---|---|---|
| **Objects and machines** | poses, liveries, what still runs, what each holds | the world |
| **Machine text** (scan pages, manifests, protocol readouts) | task, status, why it stopped. Plain and terse, e.g. "06 · RELAY · EARTH SIGNAL ON STATION FREQUENCY → STATION BUS · CELL 0%" / "06 · CHUYỂN TIẾP · TÍN HIỆU TRÁI ĐẤT TRÊN TẦN SỐ TRẠM → BUS TRẠM · PIN 0%" | machine log |
| **The masts' letters** | the official side: the closure, "not worth returning to" | Houston to the station |
| **The crew's channel** (the mare archive) | twenty years of the crew writing to each other: Ines's appeal, Theo's basil, Kenji's paperclip, Ro's Sundays | crew to crew |
| **Station traffic** | Lumen ↔ Meridian working messages; Ines ↔ Dr. Moreau | scientist to scientist |
| **Crew logs** | each crew member's own record, addressed to family or the station log | crew to family or the log |
| **Notes databank** | 1–2 sentences per machine, place or phenomenon | Kenji (Lumen's machines), Dr. Moreau's field notes (Meridian's), Ines (places and geology) |
| **The radio** | the weather loop, Ro's show (live at the end) | broadcast |
| **Functional text** | prompts, cards, hints | plain, native Vietnamese (`writing-style.md`) |

## 5. Key moments
1. **"Not worth returning to"** (0.5, the masts' letters), set against Ines's core log saying "anomalous" and the
   blue pebble on the shelf. The player knows something Earth didn't.
2. **The archive goes quiet** (0.6). Twenty years of the crew writing to each other, Ines's appeal refused, then
   nothing after 2067. Was the station forgotten? The cause, a dusty array, lands later and plainly.
3. **Meridian, still working** (0.7). A whole station of machines that never stopped, sorting samples for decades,
   with nobody reading. Its vault is the proof. Awe, and the sense of how close it all came.
4. **The moss under the timer lamp** (0.8). Underground, on a sealed cell, the shelter's garden kept one lamp on. The
   backup is there, and so is something green. Theo's log to his family: "If anything grows, tell me."
5. **We read you** (1.0). The transmission leaves the dish. A long silence. Ro's voice on the channel (text), then
   Houston's line: a crewed return is being planned. The station was deaf, never forgotten.
